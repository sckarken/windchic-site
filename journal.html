Notes from the field.

Practical advice for anyone organising an event in Europe from abroad.

Delegations · 5 min

Welcoming a Chinese delegation in Europe: five details that matter

Small attentions make a big difference to how a visit is remembered.

Read the note

Plan hot meals and offer Chinese options at least once a day; build in rest after long-haul flights; prepare bilingual programmes and name cards; respect hierarchy in seating and speeches; and make sure an interpreter is present at every business meeting, not only the official ones.

Planning · 4 min

What makes an event brief effective?

A good brief reduces the number of assumptions everyone has to make.

Read the note

Name the city, date, venue (if known), number of guests and the experience you want them to have. Separate what is mandatory from what is nice to have, give a budget range, and say who approves decisions. With that, a provider can quote precisely and you can compare quotes fairly.

Behind the scenes · 4 min

The hidden work behind an international event

Guests see the event. Behind it are dozens of decisions that need one clear owner.

Read the note

Equipment, staff, deliveries, venue rules, insurance and timings cross several companies and languages. The real value of coordination is clarity: one schedule, one contact per provider, written confirmations and a plan B for the critical items.

Catering · 4 min

Choosing a caterer for an event abroad

The lowest price per head is rarely the full story.

Read the note

Compare what is included: service staff, tableware, drinks, set-up, overtime and travel fees. Ask for a tasting, check dietary options, and confirm the kitchen constraints of the venue. A slightly higher quote with everything included is often the cheaper one.
